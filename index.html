    If something goes wrong

    one number covers almost all of it

    Iceland runs a single emergency number for police, fire, ambulance, coastguard and mountain rescue. It is free from any phone, works with no credit and no SIM, and the operators speak English.

    Emergency — police · fire · ambulance · rescue 112

    Install the 112 Iceland app before you fly. Its emergency button sends your GPS position with the call, which is the difference that matters on a gravel road with no landmarks. It also has a check-in button that leaves a trail rescuers can follow.

    The aurora game plan

    These dates straddle the autumn equinox — statistically one of the best northern-lights windows of the year — and 8 of your 10 nights are in dark-sky country. The routine:

    1. At dinner, check the aurora forecast and cloud-cover map at en.vedur.is. Gaps in cloud matter more than the Kp number — Kp 2–3 is already a show out here.
    2. Ask every hotel for a northern-lights wake-up call. Countryside hosts expect the request — make it at every check-in.
    3. Phone night mode, 3–10 seconds, propped on the car roof — it catches more color than your eyes do. A mini tripod earns its space in the bag.
    4. Push alerts: Hello Aurora (Icelandic) or My Aurora Forecast.

    Best dark skies → N2 · Seljalandsfoss N4 · Höfn / lagoon N6 · Mývatn N8 · Grundarfjörður — Kirkjufell under the lights is the trip photo

    The daily watch

    two checks a day, five minutes each

    Iceland weather is a wind problem, not a rain problem — and aurora hunting is a cloud problem, not a Kp problem. These are the sources locals actually use; every link opens in a new tab.

    Morning · weather & roads ~07:45 over breakfast

    en.vedur.is

    Icelandic Met Office — the national forecast, and the accurate one. Wind is in m/s: 15 m/s = hold the doors and expect a workout; 20+ m/s or an orange warning = rebuild the day around indoor stops; red = the car stays parked. Warning banners show right on the map.

    umferdin.is (road.is)

    Road Administration live map — closures, surface conditions, and measured gusts on the exact stretches you're driving. On the long days (4, 6, 8), check tomorrow's legs the night before too.

    safetravel.is

    ICE-SAR's alert feed — storm advisories, area and volcano closures. Register your travel plan once, and install the 112 Iceland app: one-tap emergency with your location, works everywhere.

    windy.com

    Visual gust animation — the supplementary check for timing photo stops and deciding which side of a headland to stand on.

    Evening · aurora ~19:00 at dinner

    vedur.is aurora forecast

    The one that matters: the Kp number plus the cloud-cover map. White gaps = clear sky — chase those. A clear-sky Kp 2–3 beats a cloudy Kp 6 every time at this latitude.

    Hello Aurora iOS / Android

    Icelandic-made push alerts plus live sightings from people actually standing outside in Iceland right now. Set the alert, phone by the bed, host knock requested as backup.

    NOAA SWPC 30-min forecast

    The US space-weather authority. The OVATION oval shows where the aurora is right now, refreshed every half hour — when the green blob drapes over Iceland, go outside.

    spaceweatherlive.com

    The nerd upgrade: live solar-wind speed and Bz. When Bz swings south (negative), a display usually follows within the hour.

    UAF Geophysical Institute

    University of Alaska's nightly + 27-day outlook — good for watching how your specific dates trend before you fly.

    Moon check: your dates wax toward a full moon ~Sep 25–26. Faint glows wash out; strong displays punch through. So lean on the alerts rather than patient sky-staring — and use the moonlit landscape as foreground.

    The rhythm — 07:45 vedur + umferdin over breakfast · drive · 19:00 cloud map + Kp at dinner · Hello Aurora alert set · host knock requested · 22:30 step outside, look north

    Field notes

    the things worth knowing before you need them
    Volcano status — Reykjanes
    The eruption series is between episodes: the last one ended August 2025, magma is re-accumulating, and another eruption is considered likely at some point. Blue Lagoon and Grindavík are open. Past eruptions haven't touched KEF flights, Reykjavík, or the Ring Road — only a small zone near Grindavík. Book the lagoon flexible and check safetravel.is the week you fly.
    Weather & packing
    4–12 °C, wind always, rain somewhere on the loop. Waterproof shell + fleece layers, hats and gloves, waterproof shoes. Swimsuits ride in the day bag for three soaks (Blue Lagoon, Earth Lagoon at Mývatn, Hofsós) — but the first two lend you a towel with the ticket, so the quick-dry towels are really for Hofsós and for Akureyri Backpackers, which charges for theirs. Raincoats for everyone at the walk-behind waterfalls.
    Photographs
    Two things shape every picture on this trip, and both are unusual. The sun never gets high. It tops out around 26° on the first day and about 22° on the last, so there is no harsh overhead light at any hour of any day. It rakes in from the east-south-east through the morning, swings due south around 13:00, and sits in the south-west all afternoon; shadows stay long from open to close. Golden hour is not an hour here, it is most of the day. The useful consequence is that you rarely wait for light — but you do have to know which way a thing faces. Skógafoss faces south and shows its rainbow in the morning. Vestrahorn only works before 10:00. Reynisfjara's columns only glow after 18:00. Those are on the stop lines.

    And the window is closing. It loses about an hour between the first morning and the last, and every day card carries its own sunrise, sunset and length — computed for that day's own bases rather than one number for the country.

    Gear, honestly: one lens you trust beats three you swap in weather, and a microfibre cloth matters more than any of them — Skógafoss, Dettifoss and the Jökulsárlón Zodiac will coat a front element in seconds. The good camera does not go into Blue Lagoon, Earth Lagoon or Krauma: silica water and steam finish electronics, phones live in pouches, and the other people in the water did not agree to be in your photograph. Same restraint at Hofsós.

    For the aurora, a phone on night mode genuinely works now. Anything better wants the camera braced against something solid — a wall, a fence post, the car roof — at 3 to 10 seconds, ISO 1600–3200, with manual focus set to infinity while you can still see to do it, and the flash off. The Kp strip and the moon caveat are in the aurora card.

    The ◈ line under a stop is the picture worth having there.
    Driving
    Hold car doors against the wind — the number-one rental damage claim in Iceland. Single-lane bridges in the southeast: first to arrive has right of way. Sheep own the road. 90 km/h max, headlights always on, zero-tolerance DUI. Check road.is each morning; no 4x4 needed on this route, but take gravel and sand/ash protection on the rental.
    Ocean safety — Reynisfjara & Djúpalónssandur
    Both beaches have genuinely dangerous sneaker waves. Obey the warning-light system at Reynisfjara, never turn your back on the sea, stay off the wet sand — and brief the 11-year-old hard: sneaker waves outrun sprinting adults.
    If a day goes sideways, trim these first
    Weather will steal at least half a day somewhere. Protect the booked tours first — Katla, the horses, the whale watch and Into the Glacier — then cut in this order: the Siglufjörður loop (go straight down Route 1 — saves ~2.5 hrs), the Fagradalsfjall hike, then the 15-minute stops — Grjótagjá, Petra's Stones, Fontana. Day 10 carries its own wet-weather list in the day card.
    Taking it home
    Each day card names the shop worth stopping at. What is actually worth carrying: a lopapeysa, the wool sweater — hand-knitted ones carry the knitter's name on the label, machine-made imports don't, and that is the whole test. Icelandic wool by the skein if anyone knits. Omnom chocolate and Nói Síríus liquorice, both made here. Saltverk flaky sea salt, boiled with geothermal heat. Harðfiskur, the dried fish, which is a genuinely Icelandic thing to hand someone and a genuinely divisive one. Brennivín, the caraway schnapps. Skincare from the Blue Lagoon or Earth Lagoon, both of which you will have been in. Skip the puffin keyrings and anything labelled "Nordic" rather than Icelandic — a lot of the cheap souvenir stock is imported.

    Tax free. Spend ISK 12,000 or more on one receipt in one shop and you can reclaim the VAT. Ask for the tax-free form at the till, keep the receipt stapled to it, and keep the goods reachable rather than at the bottom of a case. At Keflavík the refund desk is in the arrival hall opposite the car rentals — which is where the rental shuttle drops you — and it has to be done before you check the bags. Over ISK 100,000 needs a customs stamp first, so leave more time.

    Duty free on the way out is the cheapest alcohol and chocolate in the country, and Icelanders shop it on arrival rather than departure. Alcohol anywhere else is Vínbúðin only — the state shops, closed Sundays and early on Saturdays, and not sold in supermarkets at all.
    Food, fuel & phones
    N1/Orkan stations all along the route, and the rural ones are unmanned but open 24/7 — they need a card with a PIN, not a tap, and an N1 Prepaid Card sidesteps the question entirely. Every day card names that day's fuel and grocery stop; Bónus is the cheap chain, but its hours vary by branch and the rural ones shut earlier than you expect. Grocery stops (Bónus, Krónan, Nettó) for picnic lunches keep costs sane; restaurant meals run $25–45 a head. Langoustine night is Höfn, at Pakkhús — which takes no reservations, so arrive rather than plan. Every day card carries a lunch and a dinner pick; late September thins the rural kitchens, so ring the same morning for anything that matters and book Pakkhús, Bjargarsteinn and Strikið ahead. eSIM from Nova or Síminn, or carrier roaming — Ring Road coverage is excellent.
